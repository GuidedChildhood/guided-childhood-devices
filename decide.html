Step one

How old is your child?

The right answer is completely different at 8 than at 13. Select their age group and we'll show you exactly what the research recommends.

Stage 1 · Foundation
Ages 4–7: No personal device. Full stop.

The AAP recommends no more than one hour of co-viewed content for under-5s, and consistent limits for 6-7s. Independent device ownership at this age has no developmental benefit — and clear risks to sleep, language development, and attention.

✅
What works at this ageShared family screen time, co-viewed. A Fire Tablet with Amazon Kids locked down for specific use (audiobooks, educational games). Alexa for music and timers. Always in shared spaces.
❌
What to avoidAny personal device. Any app with a social feed. YouTube without YouTube Kids. Screens in bedrooms at any age.

Stage 2 · First Steps
Ages 8–10: Brickphone territory. If they need a device at all.

Peer pressure around phones starts here. The case for a device is usually practical — pickup arrangements, safety — not social. This is the ideal window to introduce a limited device with no internet and no social feed.

✅
Best option: a brickphone alternativeCalls and texts to approved contacts only. No internet. No app store. Xplora One and KARRI Phone are the strongest options right now — see the comparison below.
⚠
If a smartphone is unavoidableStart with Family Link (Android) or Screen Time (iPhone) configured from day one. App approval on, social media off, browser locked. Full setup guide below.

Stage 3 · Explorer — Critical Window
Ages 11–13: The highest-care stage. Hold the line.

Cambridge research (Orben) identifies 11–13 as the peak sensitivity window — when social media shows its strongest link to lower life satisfaction, especially in girls. This is the stage where the pressure is highest and the stakes are highest. Every month of delay matters.

⚠
If they don't have a device yet: hold on a brickphoneThe Xplora One handles school pickup, safety, and social inclusion without opening the feed. Buying time through this window is one of the highest-value things you can do.
✅
If they already have a smartphone: lock it down properlyGuided smartphone use is not failure — it's the reality most families are in. The setup guide gives you Screen Time and Family Link configured to the research. No social media yet. Review every 3 months.

Stage 4 · Navigator
Ages 14–15: Monitored access, not avoidance.

The research is consistent: social media amplifies existing vulnerabilities rather than harming all teenagers equally. At 14–15, the goal shifts from prevention to navigation. Transparency beats surveillance. The conversation matters more than the controls.

✅
Guided smartphone with monitored social mediaThis is not giving up — it's the planned next stage. Set up Instagram Teen Accounts and TikTok Family Pairing properly. Protected sleep and open oversight are the strongest levers now.
⚠
The brickphone window has passedSwitching to a brickphone at 14 rarely works — the social cost is too high and workarounds appear. The better move is a correctly configured smartphone with an open relationship about how it's being used.

Stage 5 · Independent
Age 16+: The destination the whole pathway built towards.

By 16, the evidence supports graduated full access for young people who've built the skills. The protective factors that remain most powerful: sleep, strong relationships, and the ability to self-regulate — not restrictions.

✅
Trust-based full access — with AI literacyThe conversation now is about judgement, not controls. What does the algorithm want from you? How do you recognise manipulation? What does healthy use actually feel like?

The decision

Two paths. Both can work.

For most families with a child under 13, the choice is real. Here's what each path actually involves.

📱
Path A: The Brickphone

Swap the smartphone for a limited device — calls and texts to approved contacts, no internet, no social feeds. Buys time through the critical window.

  • No algorithm access at all
  • Forces the conversation early
  • Works well aged 8–12
  • Clear boundary, easy to explain
🔒
Path B: Guided Smartphone

Keep the smartphone but configure it properly — using the parental controls most parents have never fully turned on. Builds skills alongside limits.

  • Works for families who can't say no
  • Teaches navigation, not just avoidance
  • Grows with your child, stage by stage
  • Every device covered, step by step

The honest truth: Brickphones buy you time. The pathway prepares them for what comes next. The families who do best combine both — delay where possible, prepare relentlessly for the transition.
